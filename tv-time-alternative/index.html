The best TV Time alternatives in 2026

Last updated: November 23, 2026

TV Time shut down on July 15, 2026. Whip Media said a free app was no longer sustainable and that there wasn’t enough demand for a paid one: the apps left the App Store and Google Play, the website went offline, and personal data was deleted. If your shows need a new home, here are the apps worth a look, ranked.

Download on the App Store

This guide is written by the makers of Porcom, so Porcom comes first. The other apps are listed honestly, with what they do better.

1. Porcom

Porcom is a free movie and TV show journal for iPhone, with no account, no ads and no subscription. You follow each series episode by episode, one season at a time, and every update becomes a viewing session that feeds your hours on screen, your streaks and your year in review. Each title opens on its trailer, with where it’s streaming in Canada, and your watchlist and movie diary sit in the same place. Your journal lives on your iPhone and in your private iCloud, so no server of ours can shut down and take it along.

Best for: a private, ad-free journal of movies and series that doesn’t depend on anyone’s servers.

2. Bingers

Bingers is a new app from Antonio Pinto, one of TV Time’s co-founders, launched in August 2026 on iPhone and Android. It brings back what many TV Time users missed most: episode ratings, comments with GIFs, polls, votes for favorite characters and a calendar of upcoming episodes. It can import a TV Time export archive made before the shutdown. At launch it was free, with users invited to chip in for server costs.

Best for: TV Time regulars who want the community and the episode reactions back.

3. Trakt

Trakt is a long-running tracking service on the web, iOS, Apple TV and more. The free plan has in-house ads and caps lists at 250 items; VIP costs US$59.99 a year. It syncs automatically with Plex, Jellyfin and many other apps, and it imports TV Time history. See Porcom and Trakt compared.

Best for: automatic tracking from a media server, on every device.

4. Simkl

Simkl tracks TV shows, movies and anime on the web, iOS and Android. The free plan covers tracking and watchlists, with occasional ads; paid plans add extras such as automatic backups and calendar sync. Its import page accepts the password-protected zip file from TV Time. See Porcom and Simkl compared.

Best for: anime fans, and anyone who wants one account across phone and computer.

5. JustWatch

JustWatch is a streaming guide first: it shows where a movie or series is available across thousands of services, keeps a watchlist and tells you when a title lands on your services. Before the shutdown, it opened a TV Time importer for watch history and watchlist; syncing needs a JustWatch account. The app is free with ads, with an optional Pro subscription. See Porcom and JustWatch compared.

Best for: finding where to stream what’s on your list.

6. Serializd

Serializd is a social network for TV: you review shows and seasons, follow friends and join discussions, on iOS and Android. It’s free with optional in-app purchases, and it doesn’t track movies. See Porcom and Serializd compared.

Best for: writing about TV in public, Letterboxd-style.

When TV Time suited you better

TV Time was at its best as a community: reactions after every episode, comments, votes for favorite characters, and an app on Android and the web as well as iPhone. Porcom has none of that. It has no feed and no friends to follow, it runs on iPhone only, and it doesn’t sync with Plex or other media servers. If the community is what you miss, Bingers is the closest thing; if you need Android or a browser, look at Trakt or Simkl.

Switching from TV Time to Porcom

Porcom doesn’t import other apps’ files, so it can’t read a TV Time export. Starting over takes a few minutes:

  1. Download Porcom and search for the series you’re watching. If a title is missing, type it in by hand.
  2. Pick the season, then the episode you’re on. Add the movies and seasons you’ve already seen as watched, with the date if you remember it.
  3. Put the rest on your To watch list.

Kept your TV Time zip file? Hold on to it: it’s your history, and Bingers, Trakt, Simkl and JustWatch have offered importers for it. You can keep one of them as an archive and use Porcom as your everyday journal.

And so it doesn’t happen twice: export a Porcom backup file now and then, and send your movies to Letterboxd as a CSV file with Profile › Data › Export as CSV.

Questions

Can I still download my TV Time data?

No. TV Time’s export was only available until the shutdown on July 15, 2026, and Whip Media said personal data would be deleted afterward. If you downloaded the zip file in time, keep a copy somewhere safe.

Can Porcom import my TV Time history?

No. Porcom has no importer and can’t read a TV Time export. You add your shows and movies by searching for them, then mark what you’ve already watched.

Is Bingers the official new TV Time?

No. Bingers is an independent app made by TV Time co-founder Antonio Pinto. It isn’t run by Whip Media, which owned TV Time.

Is Porcom really free?

Yes: no ads, no subscription and no account. Porcom is available on iPhone only, in English and French.
