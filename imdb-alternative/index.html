The best IMDb alternatives for tracking what you watch

Last updated: November 23, 2026

IMDb is the place to look up a cast list or settle a debate about a release date, and its app also lets you keep a Watchlist, rate titles, mark them as watched and check in. But all of it lives in an IMDb account, the free app shows ads, and the app is built around a huge database rather than around your own viewing. If what you really want is a journal of what you watch, these apps are made for it.

Download on the App Store

This guide is written by the makers of Porcom, so Porcom comes first. The other apps are listed honestly, with what they do better.

1. Porcom

Porcom is a free movie and TV journal for iPhone, with no account, no ads and no subscription. Your Watchlist becomes a to-watch list (plus a Spotted list for titles you saw in a trailer), series are tracked episode by episode, and every movie lands in a dated diary with your verdict: 👎 Meh, 👍 Liked, ❤️ Loved it! or 🔥 Devoured!, plus optional star ratings by theme. Each title opens on its trailer and shows where it’s streaming in Canada, and your hours on screen add up to a year in review. Everything stays on your iPhone and in your private iCloud.

Best for: iPhone users who want a private, ad-free log of movies and series instead of a public profile.

2. Letterboxd

Letterboxd is a social network for film lovers on iOS, Android and the web: a diary, ratings, reviews, lists and followers. It accepts IMDb’s CSV export files, so your IMDb history can move over. It doesn’t track TV series yet, and it’s free with ads, with paid Pro and Patron plans. Porcom vs Letterboxd.

Best for: movie lovers who want to rate, review and follow friends.

3. Trakt

Trakt tracks movies and series by episode, and syncs with media centers like Plex and Jellyfin. It has an importer for IMDb and Letterboxd history, and runs on iOS, Apple TV, the web and more. The free plan has ads and limits lists to 250 items; VIP costs US$59.99 a year. Porcom vs Trakt.

Best for: people who want automatic tracking from their media center.

4. Simkl

Simkl tracks TV shows, movies and anime, with a mobile app, a website and browser extensions. Its free plan can import watch history from IMDb and other services, and shows occasional ads; paid plans remove them. Porcom vs Simkl.

Best for: anime fans and anyone who wants one account across many services.

5. JustWatch

JustWatch is a streaming guide first: it shows where a title is available across thousands of providers and alerts you when something on your watchlist arrives on your services. It runs on iPhone, Apple TV, Android and the web, is free with ads, and offers a paid Pro option. Porcom vs JustWatch.

Best for: deciding where to watch, more than remembering what you watched.

When IMDb suits you better

Keep IMDb if you mostly look things up: who played whom, what else a director made, trivia, release dates and the famous user ratings. It’s also the better choice if you use Android or a browser, want your Watchlist on every device through one account, or like sharing public check-ins. Porcom doesn’t try to be a database or a social network.

Switching from IMDb, or using both

Porcom has no importer, so nothing moves over automatically. Before you start, export your IMDb lists as CSV from the website so you keep a copy. Then add the titles you care about in Porcom by searching for them (data from TMDB), or type a title in by hand: start with your Watchlist and the series you’re in the middle of. Many people keep IMDb for looking things up and Porcom for their own journal.

Your Porcom journal stays portable: Profile › Data › Export a backup saves a file you can restore on another iPhone, and Profile › Data › Export as CSV exports your watched movies in Letterboxd’s import format.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import my IMDb ratings or watchlist into Porcom?

Not automatically: Porcom has no importer. You add titles by searching for them (data from TMDB) or by typing them in by hand. Export your IMDb lists as CSV first so you keep a copy.

Do I need an account to use Porcom?

No. There is no sign-up and no password. Your journal is stored on your iPhone and in your private iCloud, and the developer never receives it.

Does Porcom track TV series by episode, like IMDb check-ins?

Yes. A series is tracked one season at a time, episode by episode, and each update is saved as a viewing session that feeds your hours, streaks and year in review. Porcom is private, so there is nothing public to share unless you send an image or text yourself.

Is Porcom available on Android or the web?

No. Porcom is an iPhone app only. If you need Android or a browser, Letterboxd, Trakt or Simkl are better fits.
